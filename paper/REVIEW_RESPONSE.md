# Reviewer comment audit — 14 October 2026

This is a historical audit of analyses preceding the frozen evaluation. Validation and multilink test each include both classes: the former has one session per class, and the latter has two captures per class. One label per capture does not mean a partition has only one class or invalidate evaluation across held-out captures. The remaining caveat is narrower: adjacent minutes share capture conditions, and within-capture transitions were not tested. This audit distinguishes the **primary E1/E2 model comparison** (273 real training minutes, 192 validation minutes, four multilink test captures) from the **separate occupancy model benchmark** (1,081 usable real training minutes, an internal fit/validation split, and a label-balanced 160-minute holdout). The benchmark's raw 192-minute test set is **exactly the same recordings as primary E2 validation**; its 160-minute balanced holdout is a subset, and the LLM pilot uses 16 of those minutes. These are different model analyses, not independent evaluation cohorts. The primary comparison uses no synthetic training rows. Its 16 displayed configurations were filtered using both validation and multilink test results, so they are descriptive findings rather than prospective model-selection estimates.

The companion files in `E2/outputs/revision/reviewer_audit/` were produced by `E2/audit_reviewer.py` from saved tables, caches, and logs, without retraining. `augmentation_lineage.csv` records every synthetic parent's source and mixup partner; `audit.json` summarizes assertions. The saved PCA objects report 43,500 CSI and 37,314 radar fit seconds, each matching a recount of real fit minutes. The cache was loaded with a newer scikit-learn version than the one used to save it; this count check does not establish bitwise equivalence of the PCA fit.

## Primary design limitation

The multilink captures contain 60 and 120 empty minutes and 60 and 120 occupied minutes, but **each capture has a single label throughout** (`multilink_capture_class_counts.csv`). The 192-minute validation cohort similarly consists of one occupied and one empty session. A classifier could therefore distinguish collection conditions instead of occupancy. More windows or random seeds cannot resolve this. The paper now states this limitation explicitly; a strong occupancy-transfer claim needs new mixed-class captures across repeated sessions and placements.

## Same-code augmentation ablation

`E2/no_augmentation_ablation.py` retrains the five-second RF/CSI and RF/MoE benchmark at seeds 42–46 with the original 300-tree RF configuration, frozen original-minute split, validation-fitted fusion gate, and the same label-balanced 160-minute holdout. It runs both with and without synthetic fit minutes. The saved per-minute probabilities, metrics, and fitted weights are in `E2/outputs/revision/reviewer_audit/`.

| Model | Median accuracy with 601 synthetic minutes | Median accuracy with real fit minutes only | Mean paired difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| RF/CSI | .9525 | .7500 | .2025 |
| RF/MoE | .9725 | .6725 | .3000 |

These are paired five-seed differences on one fixed, balanced, session-confounded cohort. Seed variation is not uncertainty across new recording sessions. The rerun reproduces the historical augmented **median-calibrated means**, while some per-seed standard deviations and gate weights differ slightly from the older archive because the saved analysis and current Python dependencies are not identical. The ablation's two-means column uses an exact one-dimensional split rather than the historical iterative k-means, so those columns should not be compared as identical protocols. No causal claim about augmentation or deployment follows from this ablation alone.

`E2/audit_paired_errors.py` derives exact same-minute disagreement counts from those saved probabilities. With augmentation, RF/MoE corrects 3, 5, 7, 3, and 4 RF/CSI errors across seeds 42–46, while RF/CSI corrects 1, 1, 1, 1, and 2 RF/MoE errors. Without augmentation, RF/CSI has more unique correct predictions than RF/MoE in every seed. These are paired counts on one cohort, not capture-level confidence intervals.
