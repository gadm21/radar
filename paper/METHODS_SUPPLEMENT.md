# Saved processing specification and unresolved acquisition details

This describes the separate occupancy benchmark implemented in `E2/occupancy_pipeline.py`. It is a code-derived methods record, not evidence that the hardware was configured or synchronized as intended. The primary E1/E2 comparison reuses real physical descriptor windows and has no augmentation.

## CSI timing and missing seconds

Complex CSI is converted to amplitude on 52 retained subcarriers. Packet timestamps are expressed relative to each recording's `start_ts`; samples outside `[0, 60)` seconds, nonfinite samples, and duplicate timestamps are discarded. The upstream equal-interval routine bins/interpolates onto a 100 Hz output grid. This is **100 resampled values per second**, not a claim of 100 independent received packets.

A second is accepted only when it contains at least five original packets, the first-to-last span is at least 0.5 s, the maximum gap including second boundaries is at most 0.5 s, and exactly 100 resampled values fall in that second. Invalid seconds retain a gap in the 60-second grid. The 104-dimensional per-second dot is the 52-subcarrier amplitude mean followed by population standard deviation. For PCA projections, each dot dimension is linearly interpolated across invalid seconds; leading and trailing gaps take the nearest valid dot. For rolling variance, missing amplitude rows take the **next** finite row, with trailing rows taking the last finite row. This uses future observations and is not a causal fill. A five-second CSI feature window requires at least 80% valid native seconds; the valid-second mask and the imputed products serve different purposes and must not be conflated.

The current cache records packet counts, valid-second counts and native packet rates. A final acquisition report still needs measured clock offset/drift between CSI and radar, host versus device timestamp origin, and the number and duration of gaps per recording. The source does not establish those hardware facts.

## Radar map construction

Only radar payloads of 36,864 bytes with relative timestamps in `[0, 60)` are retained. Each decoded frame is reshaped to 64 chirps × 128 samples × 3 receivers. The sample-axis mean is removed for each chirp/receiver, followed by a 128-point Hann window and range FFT; the first 64 range bins are kept. A 64-point Hann window and shifted Doppler FFT follow. With complex spectrum `D`, the pre-compression range–Doppler map is the receiver mean `(|D_1|² + |D_2|² + |D_3|²)/3`. The first two range bins are zeroed, then `log(1 + power)` is resized by linear interpolation from 64 × 64 to 24 × 24. The stored 12 × 12 map for deep models follows the same power/log computation and resizing.

For range–azimuth and range–elevation, receiver 3 is the reference. The other receiver is 1 or 2 respectively. A 25-angle grid spans −60° to 60°. For each range bin and angle, the code forms a nonnegative two-receiver steering-energy projection from individual powers and their complex cross term, averaged over chirps. The first two range bins are zeroed, `log1p` is applied, and the result is resized from 64 × 25 to 24 × 24. The XY map projects the **uncompressed RA energy** onto a normalized 24 × 24 polar-to-Cartesian grid, taking the maximum where bins collide, then applies `log1p`. Physical range calibration and antenna geometry were not available in the saved methods record, so normalized XY pixels should not be described as measured locations.

The feature named `snr_mean` is a **peak-to-background proxy**, not calibrated receiver SNR. On the resized RD map, code first uses `expm1` to return to an approximate power scale. It masks Doppler rows 10–13 and the first two range columns when finding the peak, but computes the denominator as the median of **all** map pixels. It reports `10 log10(peak / median)` dB per frame; window `snr_mean` averages these frame values. Map descriptors are the spatial mean, spatial 90th percentile, temporal 90th percentile of pixel standard deviation, mean absolute adjacent-frame difference, and spatial maximum of the frame-mean map.

Frame availability, rather than a nominal 50 ms schedule, determines window inclusion: at least five retained radar frames per five-second window or eight per ten-second window. Radar PCA variance additionally needs at least 80% of seconds with a radar frame, with per-second RD maps interpolated across missing seconds before projection. The saved benchmark median rates are 6.17 Hz for real fit empty minutes, 8.50 Hz for real fit occupied minutes, and 9.50 Hz for both holdout classes (`reviewer_audit/observed_rate_medians.csv`). The reason for this shortfall from 20 Hz is not established by the cache; a device/host timestamp and rejection audit is still required.

## Inference protocol

The benchmark internal split is random by minute within placement and class. It occurs before augmentation; PCA fits real fit minutes only. Empty fit minutes are augmented until real-plus-synthetic empty and real occupied each number 760. The test holdout is reduced from 112 empty/80 occupied to 80/80 using labels. Median and two-means thresholds are then estimated from the probabilities of that same selected cohort. These are retrospective batch-calibration results.

The primary E2 study is a different model analysis: it trains on 273 real minutes, uses 192 validation minutes, and reports four separate multilink captures. Its validation IDs are exactly the benchmark's raw 192-minute test IDs; the benchmark's balanced 160-minute holdout is a subset, not independent corroboration. E2 standardizes some evaluation batches using their own unlabeled statistics. Its `>70%` inventory uses both validation and test outcomes as a descriptive filter. Validation has one session per class, and multilink test has two captures per class. Thus both partitions evaluate both classes, although labels do not change within a capture and capture-specific effects cannot be excluded by these data alone. New mixed-class sessions would additionally evaluate within-capture transitions.
